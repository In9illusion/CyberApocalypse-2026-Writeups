# CyberApocalypse-2026-Writeups
Just wrapped up Cyber Apocalypse CTF 2026: The Salt Crown by Hack The Box! Complete beginner • Solo • Ranked 448 / 6738 teams This was only my second CTF ever. Almost everything was learned on the fly during the competition. Super tough, but honestly one of the most rewarding experiences I’ve had so far.

---

Yo. In9illusion here :)

I’m a complete beginner. Like, actually zero.

My first CTF was Bearcat CTF back in February 2026. I somehow landed in the top 29% and felt pretty good about myself… until I realized I had no idea I was supposed to write writeups. So I just vanished. No notes, nothing. Kinda regret that now.

This Cyber Apocalypse 2026 is only my second CTF in my life. I’m still a high school student on paper (technically a freshman), but because of family and money stuff I can’t continue school the normal way. Study time is extremely limited. The only structured learning I’ve done is HTB’s CoAE path (the AI Red Team one). I’ve been on it less than a month and I’m only around 45% through. That’s it. Everything else in this CTF was pure “learn on the fly 💀”.

And holy shit, this CTF hit different.

Compared to Bearcat, the challenge variety here was insane. Way more categories, way higher difficulty ceiling, but also a surprising amount of beginner-friendly stuff. I got completely wrecked in some places and flew through others. Ranking-wise I’m sitting somewhere in the top 500 (team ranking) out of 6738+ registered teams. For someone who literally just started, I’m weirdly proud of that.

Here’s the honest breakdown of how it went:

**OSINT** This is where I felt the most natural. Finished most of them insanely fast. My logical analysis brain somehow works really well here. Only problem was that one challenge where the flag composition was pure evil and made me want to scream. Everything else felt smooth.

**Forensics** Only managed the very easy ones. Deception Strategy almost killed me — I spent a full 6 hours on it, kept comparing against existing CVEs, still couldn’t close it. Lack of experience is real. Ash-Binder Signature was a bit more doable but still tough. Soloing these while exhausted is rough.

**Web** I have never properly studied web. Tried my best anyway. Massagold made me lose my mind. I found the vulnerability location but just didn’t have the experience to finish it cleanly. Frustrating as hell.

**Pwn** Also zero foundation. Surprisingly felt a little easier than web for me. Learned Ghidra and gdb on the spot and managed to clear two very easy challenges. Not bad for pure improvisation.

**Coding** Clean sweep. Honestly the second easiest category after OSINT. I think a lot of people felt the same.

**Reversing** Same story as pwn — learn and sell immediately. Managed to solve a few. Still need way more fundamentals though.

**Crypto** Hard. But looking up real-world references wasn’t that bad, so I cleared all the easy ones and half the category overall. Ashbyte Arcade was painful (huge computation) and I dropped it under time pressure. Probably a smarter way exists.

**Blockchain** Complete skip. Zero knowledge. Lol.

**Cloud** The easy ones were actually very beginner-friendly. The harder ones (Bought Riot especially) with all the different identities and keys were extremely annoying. Experience shortage again.

**ICS** Pretty solid except the final one, Ash-Vault Interlock. I was so close… and then just couldn’t finish it. Pain.

**Hardware** Did about half. Again, pure on-the-spot learning. Picked up some nice tools along the way (dnspy, etc.). Almost everything outside AI-ML was like this for me.

**Secure Coding** Phantom Burn. I modified the code 32 times. Thirty. Two. Times. Eventually just gave up because I was dying.

**AI-ML** This felt a bit more like home ground. Relatively comfortable. Crownspire Petitions was especially fun — I’ll write a longer detailed thought process for that one later because it’s the only area I actually feel somewhat competent in lol.

**Mobile** Thank you, past me, for randomly jumping into HackerOne bug bounty with zero knowledge and accidentally learning jadx. Managed the easiest challenge. No time left for the other two.

**Quantum** Somehow… finished all of them?? They were hard, but the challenge structures weren’t overly tricky, so even a beginner like me could grind through.

**Gamepwn** Looked at some conditions, realized I was out of my depth, and peace’d out. Ability just wasn’t there.

---

Overall feeling: I only scored on the challenges I could actually touch, and that was somehow enough to land a pretty high ranking for a true beginner. I genuinely believe the fastest way for someone starting from absolute zero to understand cybersecurity is to just jump into CTFs, see every weird challenge type, adapt on the spot, and then go back and fill the huge holes you discover. It gives you direction and confidence at the same time.

I’m happy I solo’d this ranking with less than a month of half-finished AI Red Team content. Now I finally know exactly where I need to study next.

Time to actually write the real writeups now before the platform closes.

See you in the next one.
